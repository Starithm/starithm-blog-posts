## Why This Matters

Jets are among the most energetic and enigmatic phenomena in the universe, yet fundamental questions about how they form, accelerate, and dissipate energy remain unresolved. These relativistic outflows appear across an extraordinary range of cosmic scales—from the compact disks around young stars to supermassive black holes at galactic centers—and they play a central role in shaping the electromagnetic and gravitational-wave signatures we observe during transient events. A new perspective on jet physics comes from millimeter and sub-millimeter observations, which offer unique diagnostic capabilities that have been largely underutilized in the era of multi-messenger astronomy. This paper argues that next-generation mm/sub-mm interferometry will transform our understanding of jets by providing robust measurements of particle acceleration, magnetic fields, and jet structure across the full cosmic mass scale.

## What They Found

The authors present a comprehensive case for why millimeter and sub-millimeter wavelengths are essential for jet science. Unlike observations at other wavelengths, mm/sub-mm data can track the evolving synchrotron turnover—the frequency at which the jet's emission peaks—which provides *robust calorimetry and direct constraints on Lorentz factors*. This capability is particularly valuable for understanding where and how jets dissipate energy and accelerate particles to relativistic speeds.

The paper emphasizes that full-Stokes polarimetry at mm/sub-mm wavelengths, combined with Faraday rotation diagnostics, enables observations *close to the jet base*—the region where jets are launched and collimated. This is where the most fundamental physics occurs, yet it remains observationally challenging. Additionally, global Very Long Baseline Interferometry (VLBI) networks operating at these wavelengths can achieve unprecedented angular resolution, from imaging ejecta in Galactic X-ray binaries to resolving event-horizon-scale structures in active galactic nuclei.

## Why It Matters

Jets operate across systems that generate multi-messenger signals—gravitational waves from neutron star mergers, neutrinos from gamma-ray bursts, and electromagnetic radiation across the spectrum. Understanding jet physics is therefore central to interpreting the full diversity of transient phenomena. The authors argue that mm/sub-mm observations are uniquely positioned to address key open questions: How are jets launched from accretion disks? What accelerates particles to such extreme energies? How do jets couple to their environments to produce the electromagnetic counterparts we observe?

## What's Next

The paper describes capabilities that will be enabled by ALMA2040, a next-generation mm/sub-mm interferometer combining *an order-of-magnitude sensitivity gain* with rapid-response observing, simultaneous multi-band coverage, and full-Stokes polarimetry. These advances will enable *statistically controlled, trigger-driven samples* of X-ray binary outbursts, early gamma-ray burst afterglows, protostellar jets, AGN jet bases, and jetted tidal disruption events. The authors position mm/sub-mm astronomy as a cornerstone of time-domain and multi-messenger jet physics through the 2040s and beyond.
